## title: DISCORDANT DESCENDANTS — CHAPTER 2

# DISCORDANT DESCENDANTS

*Chapter 2 — The Gathering*

**by Confidence Cyrus**

---

## THE NIGHT OF THE GATHERING

The Okoye residence had never looked more intimidating.

Golden lights lined the enormous driveway.

Luxury vehicles arrived one after another.

Music drifted through the open doors of the mansion, mixing with the sound of conversation, laughter, and the occasional clinking of glasses.

Lexis stood at the bottom of the staircase.

She stared at the crowd.

“This is supposed to be a family gathering?”

Her father adjusted his jacket.

“Yes.”

Lexis looked around.

“There are enough people here to form a small government.”

He laughed.

“Just behave yourself.”

“I always behave.”

Her father gave her a look.

Lexis sighed.

“Fine. I usually behave.”

He smiled.

But the moment he looked toward the entrance, his expression changed.

Lexis noticed.

“Dad?”

He didn't answer.

Someone had just entered.

A tall woman in a dark emerald dress walked slowly into the mansion.

Several conversations stopped.

Lexis frowned.

“Who is that?”

Her father looked at her.

“You don't need to know.”

That answer immediately made Lexis more interested.

“Then obviously I need to know.”

“Lexis.”

“What?”

“Stay close to me tonight.”

She stared at him.

“You're making this sound incredibly normal.”

Before he could respond, a voice came from behind them.

“Well, well.”

Sirena.

She smiled.

“Look who finally arrived.”

Lexis turned.

“Sirena.”

Sirena's eyes moved briefly toward Lexis's father.

Then toward Lexis.

“Enjoying the evening?”

“Ask me again when I know what I'm supposed to be enjoying.”

Sirena laughed.

“You'll understand soon enough.”

She walked away.

Lexis watched her go.

Then she looked at her father.

“Okay.”

She folded her arms.

“What is happening?”

Her father didn't answer.

Instead, he looked toward the staircase.

The music stopped.

Everyone turned.

An elderly man stepped onto the balcony above them.

The room fell completely silent.

“Ladies and gentlemen,” he said.

“Tonight, the Okoye family welcomes its next heir.”

Lexis's heart skipped.

Her father slowly reached for her hand.

And for the first time that night—

he looked afraid.

---

## THE ANNOUNCEMENT

The elderly man continued.

“For generations, this family has protected its legacy through one simple principle.”

He paused.

“Blood remembers what people forget.”

A murmur passed through the room.

Lexis frowned.

“What does that even mean?”

Her father squeezed her hand.

“Listen.”

The man raised an old envelope.

It was black.

And on the front—

was the crest.

The same crest Lexis had seen in her father's study.

Her eyes widened.

“That envelope…”

Her father whispered.

“Don't.”

Lexis looked at him.

“You know what that is.”

“Lexis.”

“You told me it wasn't important.”

“I told you that because I was trying to protect you.”

She stared at him.

“From what?”

Before he could answer, the elderly man spoke again.

“There has been a misunderstanding concerning the identity of the next heir.”

The room erupted in whispers.

Lexis felt her stomach tighten.

The man looked directly toward her.

“Miss Lexis Azunna.”

Silence.

Lexis froze.

Her father closed his eyes.

“No.”

The elderly man's voice remained calm.

“You are requested to come forward.”

Lexis looked around the room.

Every eye was on her.

Sirena was smiling.

Not happily.

Knowingly.

Lexis whispered:

“Dad…”

Her father looked at her.

“You have two choices.”

Lexis blinked.

“What?”

“You can walk forward.”

He paused.

“Or you can leave.”

Lexis looked toward the entrance.

Then toward the balcony.

Then toward Sirena.

Something about the entire evening felt rehearsed.

Someone had expected her to be here.

And that frightened her more than the announcement itself.

Lexis slowly released her father's hand.

“Fine.”

She stepped forward.

One step.

Then another.

The room seemed impossibly quiet.

She stopped beneath the balcony.

The elderly man studied her.

“Lexis Azunna.”

“Yes?”

“You carry the bloodline of the First Heir.”

A murmur moved through the room.

Lexis frowned.

“I'm sorry?”

The man opened the black envelope.

Inside was an old photograph.

He held it up.

Lexis stared.

The woman in the photograph was the same mysterious person from Sirena's photograph.

The woman whose face had been deliberately obscured.

Except this time—

her face was visible.

Lexis's breath caught.

She looked exactly like her.

Not similar.

Not vaguely familiar.

Exactly.

Lexis whispered:

“Who is she?”

The elderly man looked toward her father.

Her father said nothing.

Then Sirena stepped forward.

“She was your father's sister.”

Lexis turned sharply.

“My what?”

Sirena smiled.

“Your aunt.”

Lexis looked at her father.

“You told me I had no aunt.”

Her father's silence answered her.

The room suddenly felt much smaller.

[CONSEQUENCE:B]

Lexis turned toward the entrance.

“No.”

Her father grabbed her wrist gently.

“Lexis, wait.”

She pulled her hand away.

“You knew this was going to happen.”

“Not everything.”

“But enough.”

She walked toward the door.

Sirena called after her.

“Running away won't change who you are.”

Lexis stopped.

She turned.

“And neither will lying to me.”

She left the mansion.

Outside, the night air felt colder.

She pulled out her phone.

Three unread messages.

The first was from the unknown number.

*You chose correctly.*

The second appeared immediately.

*Now go home.*

The third arrived before she could open the others.

*And whatever you do, don't trust the woman in green.*

Lexis slowly looked back at the mansion.

The woman in the emerald dress stood in the doorway.

Watching her.

[ENDCHOICE]

## THE WOMAN IN GREEN

Lexis returned to the mansion.

She didn't know why.

Maybe curiosity.

Maybe anger.

Maybe because leaving had suddenly made even less sense than staying.

She entered through the side door.

The party had resumed.

But something had changed.

People were whispering.

Watching her.

Judging her.

Or perhaps—

waiting for her.

Lexis looked toward the woman in green.

She was gone.

“Of course.”

Lexis walked through the corridor.

She noticed several closed doors.

One had a small brass plaque.

**PRIVATE ARCHIVE**

Lexis stopped.

Her father's voice came from behind her.

“Don't.”

She turned.

“You really need to stop telling me not to do things.”

“This isn't a game.”

“I know.”

“No, Lexis.”

His voice softened.

“You don't.”

She looked at him.

“What happened to her?”

He stared at the floor.

“Your aunt disappeared twenty years ago.”

Lexis's expression changed.

“And you never told me?”

“She wasn't supposed to come back.”

Lexis froze.

“Come back?”

Her father looked toward the corridor.

Then lowered his voice.

“She's here.”

Lexis stared at her father.

“Tell me everything.”

He looked exhausted.

“Your aunt was the first person chosen to inherit the Okoye legacy.”

“The First Heir.”

He nodded.

“She refused.”

“Why?”

“Because she discovered what the family had been hiding.”

Lexis waited.

Her father looked toward the windows.

“There was another child.”

Lexis frowned.

“Another child?”

“Someone who was erased from the family records.”

“Why?”

“Because the family believed that person threatened the inheritance.”

Lexis's voice became quieter.

“Who was the child?”

Her father looked directly at her.

“I don't know.”

Lexis narrowed her eyes.

“You're lying.”

He didn't deny it.

Instead, he reached into his jacket and removed a folded photograph.

He handed it to her.

Lexis opened it.

Three people stood outside the Okoye estate.

Her grandfather.

Her aunt.

And a little girl.

On the back of the photograph were four handwritten words:

**THE SECOND LINE LIVES.**

Lexis stared at the message.

Then at her father.

“What does that mean?”

He whispered:

“It means the First Heir wasn't the end of the story.”

[ENDCHOICE]

[CONSEQUENCE:B]

Lexis waited until her father turned away.

Then she slipped through the door marked PRIVATE ARCHIVE.

The room was dark.

Shelves covered every wall.

Old records.

Photographs.

Boxes.

Documents.

Lexis turned on her phone flashlight.

“This family seriously needs a filing system.”

She searched the nearest shelf.

Nothing.

Another shelf.

Nothing.

Then she saw it.

A black leather file.

The crest was stamped onto the cover.

Lexis pulled it free.

Inside were several photographs.

Family records.

Newspaper clippings.

And one handwritten page.

She read the first sentence.

**THE FIRST HEIR MUST NEVER LEARN ABOUT THE SECOND LINE.**

Lexis stopped breathing for a moment.

“What?”

A floorboard creaked behind her.

She turned.

Nobody.

She looked back at the file.

The page was gone.

Lexis searched the folder.

Nothing.

Then her phone vibrated.

Unknown number.

*You shouldn't have entered the archive.*

Lexis stared at the screen.

Another message appeared.

*But now that you have…*

A final message appeared.

*Look beneath the third shelf.*

Lexis slowly turned toward the shelves.

There were dozens.

She counted.

One.

Two.

Three.

Her fingers touched the floor beneath the third shelf.

Something was there.

A small metal key.

[ENDCHOICE]

---

## THE KEY

Lexis held the key between two fingers.

It was old.

Heavy.

And engraved with the same crest.

She heard footsteps approaching.

Someone was coming.

She slipped the key into her pocket.

The archive door opened.

Sirena stood there.

Lexis's expression hardened.

“Following me now?”

Sirena smiled.

“I could ask you the same thing.”

“You knew about this place.”

“Yes.”

“You knew about the archive.”

“Yes.”

“You knew about the First Heir.”

Sirena's smile disappeared.

“Not everything.”

Lexis stepped closer.

“Then tell me what you know.”

Sirena looked toward the key in Lexis's hand.

Her eyes widened.

“You found it.”

Lexis immediately closed her fingers around it.

“Found what?”

Sirena stared at her.

Then whispered:

“The door.”

Lexis frowned.

“What door?”

Sirena looked frightened for the first time.

“The one your grandfather sealed.”

Before Lexis could respond—

the lights went out.

The mansion fell completely dark.

Someone screamed downstairs.

Then came the sound of a door slamming.

Lexis reached for her phone.

The screen lit up.

One new message.

No number.

No name.

Only four words:

**THE HEIR HAS AWAKENED.**

Lexis looked at Sirena.

Sirena looked back.

Neither of them spoke.

Then—

a lock clicked somewhere beneath the floor.

Lexis looked down at the key in her hand.

The crest began to glow faintly beneath the darkness.

And somewhere inside the mansion—

something opened.
